# Fast Fashion — English Presentation Script

- Target time: about 5 minutes 20 seconds (speak slowly, about 115 words per minute)
- Level: simple junior-high-school English
- `/` = take a short pause. Blank line = take a longer pause.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

Hello, everyone. /
Today, I want to talk about fast fashion. /
The title of my presentation is "Fast Fashion: Cheap Clothes, Big Problems."

## Slide 2 — What is fast fashion?

First, / what is fast fashion? /
Where do you usually buy your clothes? /
Many of you know UNIQLO, ZARA, and H&M. /
Some of you may also buy clothes from SHEIN on the internet. /
These brands are called "fast fashion" brands.

## Slide 3 — Why "fast"?

Why do we say "fast"? /
"Fast" means "quick." /
It is the same "fast" as in "fast food." /
A long time ago, / a newspaper writer wrote about ZARA. /
ZARA needs only 15 days / to make a new design / and put the clothes in its shops. /
That is very fast! /
After that, / people started to use the words "fast fashion." /
Now, SHEIN is very popular. /
SHEIN makes and sells new clothes / in only about 10 days.

## Slide 4 — Useful, but…

Fast fashion clothes are cheap. /
They are in style, / and we can get them very quickly. /
So fast fashion is very useful for us. /
But there are big problems behind it. /
In 2013, / a building fell down in Bangladesh. /
Many people made clothes in that building. /
More than 1,000 workers died. /
Also, / factories that make clothes / give out a lot of greenhouse gases. /
People say it is about 10 percent / of all the greenhouse gases in the world.

## Slide 5 — Where do old clothes go?

Next, / where do our old clothes go? /
Some clothes we do not wear anymore. /
Some clothes are so cheap / that nobody buys them. /
Where do they go? /
In fact, / rich countries like Japan / and countries in Europe / send a lot of them to faraway countries.

## Slide 6 — Chile: the Atacama Desert

In South America, / there is a country called Chile. /
In Chile, / there is a desert called the Atacama Desert. /
Every year, / about 60,000 tons of clothes / come to Chile from around the world. /
Many of them do not sell, / and people leave them in the desert. /
The mountains of clothes are very big. /
You can see them / even from space! /
Many clothes today are made from plastic-like materials. /
So they need hundreds of years / to go back to the earth. /
And when people burn these mountains, / the smoke is bad for our bodies. /
It hurts the health of people / who live near there.

## Slide 7 — Ghana

Now, let's look at Africa. /
Ghana is a country in Africa. /
Every week, / about 15 million pieces of used clothes / come to Ghana. /
Today, / many clothes are cheap / and break easily. /
So about 40 percent of these clothes / are too bad to wear. /
They just become garbage. /
Rich countries buy a lot of clothes. /
Then they push their garbage / onto countries that cannot clean it up. /
People call this "waste colonialism." /
This garbage also makes rivers and the sea dirty.

## Slide 8 — Chile's new plan

Last, / how is the world trying to stop this? /
People say, / "The earth will be full of garbage!" /
So countries are starting to make new rules. /
Chile is cleaning up the mountains of clothes. /
Chile wants to change old clothes / into building materials / and new thread. /
Chile also made a new law. /
It says, / "Companies must collect their unsold clothes / and recycle them. / Do not throw them away."

## Slide 9 — Europe and France

In Europe, / the EU has a new rule from 2026. /
Big companies cannot throw away or burn / their unsold clothes and shoes. /
In France, / there is a new law / for companies like SHEIN. /
If their clothes are bad for the environment, / they must pay extra money. /
Also, / they cannot show ads for their clothes.

## Slide 10 — Bristol, England

In England, / there is a city called Bristol. /
Bristol stopped fast fashion ads / in the city. /
Why? /
The city does not want ads / to tell people, / "Buy more clothes!"

## Slide 11 — Summary

Let me finish. /
Our cheap clothes / have a hidden story. /
They hurt nature / and people in faraway countries. /
Now, / people around the world / are trying to change this. /
So next time you buy clothes, / please stop / and think, /
"Do I really need this?" /
Thank you for listening.
